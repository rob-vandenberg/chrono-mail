# Privacy

Chrono Mail is a desktop mail program that runs on your own computer. It has no server of its own and sends nothing about you anywhere.

## What stays on your computer

- Your mail, calendars, tasks and contacts are kept on your computer, encrypted.
- Passwords, app passwords and the codes of a Google sign-in are kept in your computer's own password store (KDE Wallet, GNOME Keyring or the Windows Credential Manager), never in a plain file.
- Chrono Mail talks only to the servers of the accounts you add: your mail servers, and your calendar and contacts servers.

## Google sign-in

When you add Google Calendar and Contacts, Chrono Mail opens Google's own sign-in page in your web browser. You sign in there; Chrono Mail never sees your Google password.

- Chrono Mail asks Google only for access to your calendars and contacts (and your e-mail address, to name the account). It never asks for access to your mail through Google sign-in.
- What Chrono Mail gets from Google stays on your computer and is used only to show and keep your calendars and contacts in step with Google.
- Nothing is sent to the makers of Chrono Mail or to anyone else, and nothing is used for advertising.
- You can take the access back at any time at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions, or by removing the account in Chrono Mail.

Chrono Mail's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

## Updates

Chrono Mail can check GitHub for a newer version, only when you ask for it or allow it. It only asks GitHub which version is the newest; it sends nothing about you or your mail.

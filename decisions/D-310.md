# Calendar messages in mail (iTIP)
Topics: [[calendars-contacts-tasks]] · [[mail-window]] · [[basic-rule]]

(The owner) **Chrono Mail supports every calendar message a mail can carry** (RFC 5546, RFC 6047; journal entries later, B-32): a mail holding one shows a card above its text, alone when the text shows nothing, never a white page, and the calendar part is not offered as a file. Answers go to a calendar the person picks (that of the receiving account first); per account the person ticks "Send confirmation emails via" with Chrono Mail or the server: unticked nothing goes out; greyed only when certain: a server tested to send them whatever it is told (Kerio) leaves no choice, no calendar server means Chrono Mail; any other server that announces it is told SCHEDULE-AGENT=CLIENT when it should stay silent; when the server cannot answer (another address, a meeting that never reached it) Chrono Mail sends. Why: the program never shows less than the mail holds, and the organizer never gets two answers. A Decline made offline: the server's copy is first made ready, the delete goes only after the server confirmed that, so the server mails or stays silent as chosen. A Decline refused twice keeps the original here with a sync conflict and tells the person calmly. Every item of a calendar text gets its block, built as the person scrolls near it; a click changes only that block. An invite is handled when you answered it, not when it merely is in a calendar: a handled invite shows Change my answer instead of the answers; after a confirmation the appointment goes, nobody is told, and the answers return.

A Decline or a Delegate is remembered on the mail, as a keyword every computer reads; a folder that keeps no keywords keeps it here only (OR-21). A copy in a calendar knows better than the mark. A file, or a text with several items, has no such mark.

Your own meeting: saving it with attendees invites them, and each change or cancellation asks whether to mail them. The box "Send confirmation emails via" covers only answers to invitations, and the box of the account that got the invite decides.

A message about your own meeting has the two states too: an answer is handled when your copy says the same, an accepted proposal when your meeting has its times, a declined proposal and a request answered by a mark on the mail. Handled shows on the card, with the buttons greyed and an (i). The sentence names who wrote the mail; a proposal shows the time now beside the time proposed.

Every calendar mail has one dialog, not the composer: a question, an optional message for all, Send and Don't send, an (i). Closing it takes the action back, nothing changes. It opens before the change: at Save of a meeting with attendees, at every answer, at Decline proposal, Send the latest version, cancelling, a request for the original invite and busy times. Accept and Tentative pick the calendar first. With the tickbox off or the server sending, no dialog; the tickbox does not cover news of your own meeting. Delegate always asks: Chrono Mail always mails the delegate. An attendee taken off your meeting gets a cancellation at Send. An invitation that must be answered and names no organizer asks the address: the mail's sender, or empty for a file. The Replied mark comes when the answer has gone: at Send, or at once when the server mails it; with nothing sent, no mark.

A declined invite is kept: a normal event with your answer on it, hidden and inert (you count as free). It goes, without asking, into the calendar of Local Folders, on this computer only: the owner approved that exception to D-193. A copy in a server calendar is removed as before; the mark stays on the mail for other computers, and an invite knows its state by finding a copy by its UID in all calendars. Deleting a declined event really deletes it, with no mail, and the invite shows as new. A declined date of a series is hidden; the series stays whole. The button Show declined shows only what you declined, greyed, in the calendars ticked. Right-click: with an organizer Accept, Tentative, Delegate, Delete; Delete of a declined date deletes that one date, never the series. Accept and Tentative make it a normal appointment again, in the calendar the person picks. A deleted single event without an organizer no longer exists.

Delete of an event someone else organizes never mails from Chrono Mail: the whole event goes the silent way, so the server mails nothing either; one date goes on the series' list of removed dates, for which no silent way is known on a server that mails whatever it is told, and what it mails is followed. That list is final: a date on it has no Restore and shows nowhere, also one the organizer removed. Delete of your own Cancelled meeting never asks about a mail, as Cancel was the moment to tell them; a server's own cancellation is followed. An event that names another organizer without listing you is self-created: nothing mails that organizer.

An update says so and shows old and new time. Your answer stands: only Update calendar. It moved: please answer again, only the four answers. Never answered: the four answers. A mail about one date of a series says so, also on its button.

The card shows the meeting's own text and an attendee's comment, as plain text.

The warning before Accept on a series names how many of your appointments it meets in the coming year, the first few with their dates, and how many more. An invitation or an .ics file may be 1 MB; the backup copies the stores and has no limit. Every block of a mail with several items knows the mail's sender, so its sentence names who wrote it. A calendar file opened from a mail in your own Sent or Drafts is your own too: no buttons, as the mail itself.

A meeting text on the card shows 250 characters and a clickable …; the click shows up to 2048, the rest stays in the data. A mark on a mail stays with it when moved, and a mark the server has not got yet is written at the next sync. When the next blocks of a card cannot be read, a calm line with Try again says so; scrolling does not ask meanwhile.

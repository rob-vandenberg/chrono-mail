# Calendar messages in mail (iTIP)
Topics: [[calendars-contacts-tasks]] · [[mail-window]] · [[basic-rule]]

(The owner) **Chrono Mail supports every calendar message a mail can carry** (RFC 5546, RFC 6047; journal entries later, B-32): a mail holding one shows a card above its text, alone when the text shows nothing, never a white page, and the calendar part is not offered as a file. Answers go to a calendar the person picks (that of the receiving account first); per account the person ticks "Send confirmation emails via" with Chrono Mail or the server: unticked nothing goes out; greyed only when certain: a server tested to send them whatever it is told (Kerio) leaves no choice, no calendar server means Chrono Mail; any other server that announces it is told SCHEDULE-AGENT=CLIENT when it should stay silent; when the server cannot answer (another address, a meeting that never reached it) Chrono Mail sends. Why: the program never shows less than the mail holds, and the organizer never gets two answers. Decline is a normal write, server first: offline it waits, a lasting refusal is a sync conflict. A delete of a copy made offline, by Delete or Change my answer: the server's copy is first made ready, the delete goes only after the server confirmed that, so the server mails or stays silent as chosen; refused twice it keeps the original here with a sync conflict and tells the person calmly. Every item of a calendar text gets its block, built as the person scrolls near it; a click changes only that block. An invite is handled when you answered it, not when it merely is in a calendar: a handled invite shows Change my answer instead of the answers; after a confirmation the appointment goes, nobody is told, and the answers return. Decline never deletes: on a declined event Change my answer asks nothing, the event stays where it is, and only the four answers come back.

A Delegate, and a Decline that left no copy in a server calendar, is remembered on the mail, as a keyword every computer reads; a folder that keeps no keywords keeps it here only (OR-21). A copy in a calendar knows better than the mark, and a copy in a server calendar needs none: it tells every computer itself, so after Delete the mail finds nothing and shows the four answers. A whole copy in Local Folders knows its mail: when it goes, so does the mark. A file, or a text with several items, has no such mark.

Your own meeting: saving it with attendees invites them, and each change or cancellation asks whether to mail them. The box "Send confirmation emails via" covers only answers to invitations, and the box of the account that got the invite decides.

A message about your own meeting has the two states too: an answer is handled when your copy says the same, an accepted proposal when your meeting has its times, a declined proposal and a request answered by a mark on the mail. Handled shows on the card, with the buttons greyed and an (i). The sentence names who wrote the mail; a proposal shows the time now beside the time proposed.

Every calendar mail has one dialog, not the composer: a question, an optional message for all, Send and Don't send, an (i). Closing it takes the action back, nothing changes. It opens before the change: at Save of a meeting with attendees, at every answer, at Decline proposal, Send the latest version, cancelling, a request for the original invite and busy times. Accept and Tentative pick the calendar first. With the tickbox off or the server sending, no dialog; the tickbox does not cover news of your own meeting. Delegate always asks: Chrono Mail always mails the delegate. An attendee taken off your meeting gets a cancellation at Send. The Replied mark comes when the answer has gone: at Send, or at once when the server mails it; with nothing sent, no mark.

An invitation in a mail that names no organizer: the dialog shows the mail's sender in an edit field, with Send, Don't send and Cancel. Send and Don't send both store that field as the organizer in the event and close the dialog and the window; Cancel or the close button closes only the dialog and changes nothing. An empty field means no organizer: the event is self-created. Send gives one mail, Don't send none, also on a server that mails whatever it is told: the organizer is then saved last, and offline the synchronisation does the same. Who sends follows Settings > Account > Edit > Calendar tab, and a mail the server sends carries no message of yours; with the tickbox off no dialog and no mail. Once answered, the copy says who the organizer is. What a server wrote in an unanswered copy by itself decides nothing: the person is asked. An .ics file with no organizer, opened from a folder, shows no answers: it is only added to a calendar you pick. Saved offline, the same goes for what is written after it: every later write goes as those two saves too, and only a later answer the server is to mail goes as it is. A move takes this along to the new calendar.

A declined event stays in the calendar it is in: your answer on it, server first, hidden and inert (you count as free), with its own changes, reminders and colour. Only an invite declined with no copy anywhere has no calendar: it goes, without asking, into the calendar of Local Folders, on this computer only: the owner approved that exception to D-193. An invite knows its state by finding a copy by its UID in your own calendars. Deleting a declined event really deletes it, with no mail, and the invite shows as new. The mail of a declined event says: You declined. It is in your Calendar (Work), marked hidden. A double click opens it like any other event, and it can be edited. An answer on all occurrences replaces every separate answer on single occurrences, for Accept, Tentative and Decline: the program does what it says; only an organizer's update keeps a date answered separately. A declined date of a series is hidden; the series stays whole. The button Show declined shows only what you declined, greyed, in the calendars ticked. Right-click: with an organizer Accept, Tentative, Delegate, Delete; Delete of a declined date deletes that one date, never the series. Accept and Tentative make it a normal appointment again that blocks your time: a server may have marked it free, so that mark is taken back. On a copy in Local Folders Accept, Tentative and Delegate ask for a calendar; on one in any other calendar they answer in place. A deleted single event without an organizer no longer exists.

Delete of an event someone else organizes never mails from Chrono Mail: the whole event goes the silent way, so the server mails nothing either; one date goes on the series' list of removed dates, for which no silent way is known on a server that mails whatever it is told, and what it mails is followed. That list is final: a date on it has no Restore and shows nowhere, also one the organizer removed. Delete of your own Cancelled meeting never asks about a mail, as Cancel was the moment to tell them; a server's own cancellation is followed. An event that names another organizer without listing you is self-created: nothing mails that organizer.

An update says so and shows old and new time. Your answer stands: only Update calendar. It moved: please answer again, only the four answers. Never answered: the four answers. A mail about one date of a series says so, also on its button.

An update you take, by Update calendar or by an answer, is copied into the event you keep, whoever changed what: a series is one record and a deleted date a note on it. Your answer, reminders, tags, colour and deleted dates stay; a deleted date stays deleted also when the series moves. A new date or time also reaches a changed date, which keeps what else is its own, and what else changed reaches it too; a changed date that is no date of the series any more goes. Your own deleted dates are no change of time, so they never ask your answer again.

A Cancel mail keeps its button: Cancel in calendar, or Cancel this date in calendar. It sets Cancelled, for an event and for a task, and the event stays; of a whole series also a changed date with no status of its own, a date with its own status keeps it. The button is greyed once it is Cancelled, also when a server did that, and the status line says Cancelled in your calendar. Chrono Mail itself never changes the calendar from a mail. What a server does by itself is followed: an unanswered copy it made is answered in place, and an event it reset shows as unanswered until you pick an answer. A copy a server reset to unanswered still carries the server's free mark of your Decline (measured on Kerio): Accept or Tentative takes that off and blocks your time again, unless the organizer's own message says free.

The card shows the meeting's own text and an attendee's comment, as plain text.

The warning before Accept on a series names how many of your appointments it meets in the coming year, the first few with their dates, and how many more. An invitation or an .ics file may be 1 MB; the backup copies the stores and has no limit. Every block of a mail with several items knows the mail's sender, so its sentence names who wrote it. A calendar file opened from a mail in your own Sent or Drafts is your own too: no buttons, as the mail itself.

A meeting text on the card shows 250 characters and a clickable …; the click shows up to 2048, the rest stays in the data. A mark on a mail stays with it when moved, and a mark the server has not got yet is written at the next sync. When the next blocks of a card cannot be read, a calm line with Try again says so; scrolling does not ask meanwhile.
